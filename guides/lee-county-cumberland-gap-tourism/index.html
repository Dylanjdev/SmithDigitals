How Lee County businesses can capture more Cumberland Gap tourism

A practical strategy for helping cross-border visitors discover, explore, and spend more time with businesses on the Virginia side of the region.

DS

Written by Dylan SmithPublished and reviewed August 27, 2026

Quick answer

Lee County does not need to create the traffic. It needs to earn a place in the trip.

Cumberland Gap already draws hundreds of thousands of visitors. Lee County businesses can capture more of that demand by becoming easier to find in Search and Maps, creating content for regional travel intent, and connecting lodging, food, attractions, retail, and events into a fuller itinerary.

The scale of the market

The tourism opportunity is already moving through the region.

Lee County, Virginia occupies a distinctive position in Appalachia. It is part of the Cumberland Gap region, where Virginia, Kentucky, and Tennessee meet and where a major national park already attracts significant travel.

According to the National Park Service, Cumberland Gap National Historical Park recorded 729,249 visits in 2024. Those visitors spent an estimated $23.973 million in communities near the park, producing $24.31 million in total economic output. The opportunity is larger than getting one tourist to stop for lunch. It is making sure visitors know the Virginia side exists and can find its businesses when they are ready to spend.

A tri-state destination

Lee County is already part of the Cumberland Gap story.

Cumberland Gap is not simply a Kentucky or Tennessee destination. The national historical park spans all three states, and the National Park Service describes it as a mountain gateway with roughly 85 miles of trails, 14,000 acres of wilderness, caves, historic places, camping, and the ability to stand in three states at once.

Lee County brings its own reasons to extend that experience. In Ewing, Wilderness Road State Park offers hiking, living-history programming, and the reconstructed Martin's Station. Virginia State Parks places it five miles west of Ewing and about ten miles east of Cumberland Gap National Historical Park.

The positioning shift

Lee County attractions do not have to compete with Cumberland Gap. They can complement it. The practical goal is to become part of the same trip.

Beyond the trailhead

A visitor keeps making decisions after leaving the park.

Travelers need food, fuel, lodging, supplies, entertainment, and something memorable to take home. They may want another activity after hiking or a comfortable stop before continuing across the region. That makes far more businesses part of tourism than the label suggests.

Stay→Eat→Explore→Shop→Stay longer

A restaurant does not have to call itself a tourism company. Neither does a campground, retailer, gas station, venue, or farm. If a traveler can spend money there, the business can participate in the local visitor economy.

Strategy 01 · Discovery

Make the business visible on Google before the visitor chooses.

A traveler may never know the business name. They search for a need, compare the nearby options, and decide from the information available in that moment. Lee County businesses need an accurate Google Business Profile with current hours, appropriate categories, original photographs, a useful description, and genuine customer reviews. The connected website should reinforce those same details.

Google says local visibility is mainly based on relevance, distance, and prominence. A business cannot change its physical distance from the searcher, but it can improve how clearly and credibly Google understands the business. For the fundamentals, see the Smith Digitals local ranking guide.

Strategy 02 · Search geography

Stop thinking only in county boundaries.

Residents and visitors often describe the same need differently. A Lee County resident may search for a “Pennington Gap restaurant,” while a traveler asks for the “best restaurant near Cumberland Gap.” Either search could lead to the same business.

Cumberland Gap → Southwest Virginia → Lee County→ Ewing → Pennington Gap

Regional thinking is not permission to stuff every place name into every page. It means explaining where the business really is, what it is near, what area it genuinely serves, and why someone traveling through the region should consider the stop. That context is useful to both customers and search engines.

Strategy 03 · Collaboration

Build connections that turn one stop into an itinerary.

The next opportunity is larger than SEO. A cabin can recommend a restaurant. A restaurant can point guests toward an attraction. An outdoor business can highlight lodging, and a downtown shop can promote the weekend's events.

Simple ways local businesses can work together

  • Create honest “nearby” recommendations on relevant website pages.
  • Assemble one-day and weekend itineraries around complementary businesses.
  • Exchange printed cards or QR links for useful visitor resources.
  • Keep event, seasonal-hours, and lodging information current across partners.
  • Use descriptive website links that help visitors continue planning.

The more businesses help visitors plan a complete experience, the less likely Lee County is to be treated as somewhere people simply drive through.

Strategy 04 · More reasons

Turn separate attractions into reasons to stay longer.

A visitor who discovers one activity can leave after completing it. A visitor who finds five compatible activities has a reason to stay. A history-focused trip can connect Wilderness Road and Cumberland Gap. An outdoor weekend can combine trails, riding, food, lodging, and local events. A family can balance recreation with entertainment and an easy meal.

The individual attractions do not have to compete. When they are presented as parts of a coherent regional experience, each can make the others more useful.

Strategy 05 · A stronger destination

Give tourists a reason to include Pennington Gap.

Pennington Gap should not have to wait for a traveler to search the town by name. The community can be presented as another worthwhile part of a broader Cumberland Gap and Southwest Virginia visit.

The Lee Theatre, historic Leeman Field Recreation Park, its greenway, Leeman Field RV Park and Campground, nearby outdoor recreation, local events, and independent businesses create multiple reasons to explore. The town's official campground information specifically connects visitors to the park, Greenway, Stone Mountain ATV Trail, downtown shops, restaurants, and entertainment.

Write for the traveler's question

“We are a restaurant in Pennington Gap” states a fact. “Looking for somewhere to eat after exploring Cumberland Gap?” connects that restaurant to a real trip-planning decision.

Businesses serving this market can also review the dedicated Pennington Gap web design page for the website foundations behind that content.

Strategy 06 · Reach visitors early

Create useful content before the traveler arrives.

By the time a tourist reaches town, many food, lodging, and activity decisions are already made. Helpful trip-planning content gives a local business the opportunity to become visible weeks or months earlier.

  • What to do near Cumberland Gap after hiking
  • Where to eat near Cumberland Gap
  • A weekend guide to Lee County, Virginia
  • Things to do in Pennington Gap, VA
  • Where to stay near Cumberland Gap on the Virginia side
  • Family-friendly things to do in Lee County

These pages should be real resources—not advertisements stretched to a word count. Include accurate travel context, useful recommendations, original knowledge, clear authorship, and an honest connection to the business.

Strategy 07 · In-the-moment demand

Do not forget the traveler passing through.

Tourism is not limited to week-long stays. People traveling along US-58 and US-421 may have an hour, need fuel, want lunch, or notice something worth visiting on Maps. A clear profile, accurate open hours, good photographs, and an easy route from discovery to directions can turn through-traffic into a customer.

Strategy 08 · The mobile storefront

Make Google Maps part of the tourism strategy.

For a traveler already in the region, Maps is both navigation and discovery. They may search only for “coffee,” “campground,” “gas,” “shopping,” or “things to do.” The profile is often the first impression: category, rating, recent reviews, photographs, hours, distance, and website all sit together.

  1. Confirm the core details

    Keep the name, category, location or service area, hours, phone number, and website accurate.

  2. Show what a visitor will experience

    Add current, original photographs of the entrance, setting, products, food, rooms, amenities, or activity.

  3. Manage seasonal changes

    Update special hours, temporary closures, events, reservation information, and time-sensitive visitor details.

  4. Earn trust honestly

    Invite genuine customer reviews and respond professionally without buying, gating, or incentivizing feedback.

Strategy 09 · Shared momentum

Build a stronger regional brand together.

No single business has to carry Lee County tourism. The county becomes easier to discover when dozens of restaurants, lodging providers, shops, venues, campgrounds, outdoor businesses, attractions, and organizations improve their digital presence and point visitors toward one another.

The Cumberland Gap region already has a major tourism asset. Lee County's opportunity is to make sure visitors understand that Virginia is part of the experience too.

Keep the numbers in context

The National Park Service figure measures spending in communities near the park; it does not identify how much reached Lee County. It also does not mean every visitor will cross into Virginia or benefit every business equally. The data proves that regional demand exists—not that capturing it is automatic.

Put the strategy to work

A practical 30-day tourism visibility plan.

Week 1

Audit the Business Profile, website, hours, categories, photographs, directions, and visitor-facing information.

Week 2

Define the real regional search opportunities and publish or improve one genuinely useful traveler page.

Week 3

Connect with three complementary local businesses and build a simple recommendation or itinerary loop.

Week 4

Track calls, directions, website visits, inquiries, and in-person questions so the next improvement follows real behavior.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

How can a Lee County business attract Cumberland Gap visitors?

Start with accurate Google Business Profile information, a fast mobile website, useful content for people planning a regional trip, and clear explanations of where the business is relative to Cumberland Gap. Local partnerships and referrals can then help visitors turn one stop into a longer Lee County itinerary.

Does my company need to be a traditional tourism business?

No. Restaurants, retailers, gas stations, entertainment venues, farms, service businesses, campgrounds, and other places where travelers can spend money may all participate in the visitor economy. The important question is whether the business offers something useful or interesting to a traveler.

Should I target Lee County or Cumberland Gap keywords?

Use the locations that accurately describe the business and the visitor's decision. A Pennington Gap restaurant can build relevance for Pennington Gap while also publishing genuinely useful content for people looking for food near Cumberland Gap. Avoid adding unrelated towns or repeating place names unnaturally.

Why does Google Maps matter for regional tourism?

Travelers often use Maps to discover nearby food, lodging, fuel, shopping, and attractions while already on the road. Complete hours, accurate categories, current photographs, genuine reviews, and a connected website make it easier for a visitor to understand and choose a business.
