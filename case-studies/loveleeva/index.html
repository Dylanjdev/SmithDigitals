Project overview

More than a storefront.

The project needed to support real commerce while telling a much larger story about what rural communities can build for themselves.

“We Grow Our Own” became the organizing idea: one clear message connecting products, infrastructure, people, and place without flattening any of them into a conventional small-business website.

The organizing idea

Four pillars. One local ecosystem.

01G

Goods

Locally made products and custom craftsmanship

02P

Power

Resilient rural infrastructure and engineering

03T

Talent

Workforce development and technical mentorship

04A

Adventure

Tourism, events, and Appalachian discovery

The challenge

Connect experiences that usually live apart.

The public experience had to feel like one brand even though the work underneath it spans retail operations, civic participation, editorial publishing, and long-range economic development.

01

Sell physical products with accurate inventory, shipping, and taxes.

02

Help residents and visitors discover local businesses.

03

Publish community events and accept moderated submissions.

04

Promote Lee County as a travel destination.

05

Explain long-term workforce and rural-development goals.

06

Create a distinct identity rooted in Appalachia.

The solution

A responsive ecosystem with a secure commerce core.

A headless React experience keeps the brand and customer journey flexible, while specialized systems handle the operational work they do best.

Beyond commerce

The community can participate, not just browse.

Discovery tools turn LoveLeeVa into shared local infrastructure—useful to residents, visitors, businesses, and event organizers alike.

01

Business directory

Search and filters make Lee County organizations easier to find, while moderated submissions and logo uploads let the directory grow with the community.

02

Community calendar

Category filters, event detail pages, individual and bulk CSV submissions, and a subscribable live feed turn scattered listings into a shared resource.

03

Local travel guides

In-depth Lee County and Cumberland Gap content gives visitors a useful starting point and sends attention toward local places and organizations.

04

Human workflows

Custom-order and general-contact paths make room for work that does not fit neatly inside a standard storefront transaction.

Design approach

Rooted in place.
Built for now.

Forest green, moss, copper, clay, cream, and gold draw from Southwest Virginia without slipping into nostalgia. Warm textures and restrained motion pair with a clear, contemporary interface.

Playfair Display, Oswald, and Lora balance editorial storytelling with the character of something made by hand.

Accessibility, built in

Responsive layouts, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, reduced-motion support, descriptive image text, and a skip link were treated as product requirements—not post-launch polish.

Technical approach

A modern stack, chosen around the work.

Public routes are prerendered so meaningful content reaches search engines before JavaScript loads. Product images move securely from Odoo through the edge, where they are converted to WebP and cached.

  • ExperienceReact 19 · React Router · Vite
  • EdgeCloudflare Workers · Cloudflare Images
  • CommerceOdoo · Stripe Elements · PaymentIntents
  • OperationsUPS · Avalara · Odoo customer portal
  • CommunitySupabase · Formspree · CSV workflows
  • DiscoveryPrerendering · structured data · sitemaps

One foundation for commerce, participation, and discovery.
