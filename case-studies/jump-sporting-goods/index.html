About the business

Serving SWVA, one order at a time.

Jump Sporting Goods has built a loyal customer base across Southwest Virginia by doing one thing really well — delivering quality custom apparel on time, with a personal touch. Every order is tailored to the organization it's made for, from school mascot tees to department insignia gear.

Their product catalog spans shirts, hats, cups, stickers, and sports equipment. Whether it's a youth baseball team needing uniforms or a local fire department ordering custom gear, Jump handles it all from their location at 164 Cross Creek, Dryden, VA.

Smith Digitals built their custom e-commerce website to make ordering easier for organizations — with a browsable product catalog organized by customer type, so schools, teams, and departments can quickly find and order exactly what they need.

Behind the build

The decisions were shaped by the business, not by a template.

Jump Sporting Goods serves organizations with different buying contexts, from schools and churches to municipalities and rescue departments. The website needed to explain a broad custom apparel offer without making every visitor sort through irrelevant information.

Design and strategy decisions

What made this project specific to the client.

01

Organize around buyers

Category paths help a school, faith organization, or department identify its use case quickly instead of beginning with a generic product wall.

02

Make custom ordering understandable

The experience gives custom apparel a clearer process so visitors understand what happens between an idea and a finished order.

03

Support high-consideration sales

The site provides enough context for a group decision while preserving direct contact for details that require a conversation.

04

Treat ecommerce as a system

The storefront, product presentation, and inquiry path were designed to work together rather than forcing a one-size-fits-all checkout model.

How the work came together

Strategy, experience, and implementation in one loop.

The build started with audience and order complexity rather than a template. Custom apparel is rarely an impulse purchase; the site therefore had to reduce uncertainty, set expectations, and create a clean handoff when a project needed specific guidance.

When one business serves several organizations, the most useful navigation is often based on the customer's job, not the company's internal departments. Clear pathways make a broad catalog feel more relevant.
